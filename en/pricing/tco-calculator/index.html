Hong Kong LMS Cost Calculator: Key Facts and Implementation Scope

What it is
A transparent arithmetic model of the six cost categories an LMS actually has. You fill in the numbers; it does the sum.
What it is not
Not a quote, not a market benchmark, and not a claim about what any vendor charges. We do not publish other vendors' prices, including estimates of them.
The license line
OpenLMS is HK$0 for the platform for every customer, so that line is zero. Every other line is real money on both sides and is included in the total.
Where the data goes
Nowhere. The calculation is JavaScript in your browser. No server call, no cookie, no analytics on this page.
What to do with the result
Use it to decide which questions to ask vendors — including us. If the commercial total is lower because your volume is small and you need no configuration, that is a real answer.
Missing an important cost?
Hosting, implementation, migration, content and operations are the six standard categories. If your situation has a seventh, ask us and we will add it.

    Hong Kong LMS Cost Calculator: Frequently Asked Questions

    Is an open-source LMS always cheaper?

    No, and we will not pretend otherwise. If you have very few users, need a working system immediately, and have nobody to run the platform, a commercial license can be the cheaper total. The saving appears at volume, over time, or where the platform has to be adapted to your processes rather than the other way round.

    Why is the platform free when you still charge for services?

    Because our commercial engine is hosted subscriptions, courseware development and implementation work — not seats. We do not need license revenue to survive, so we do not charge for it. Every price we do charge is published: HK$30 per user per year hosted, HK$30,000 for on-premises installation, HK$1,500 per person-day for customization. That is a genuinely different incentive from a vendor whose revenue depends on how many accounts you open.

    Are there hidden third-party component fees?

    The platform core is GPL open source with no per-seat or annual charge. Where an optional component is commercially licensed — for example a proprietary authoring tool or a commercial support contract from an upstream provider — we say so explicitly in the scope rather than folding it into a number. We will not present an open-source core as if it were a zero-cost total.

    What does the government funding actually cover?

    That depends on the scheme and on the scope of work you submit. The Innovation and Technology Fund lists Learning Management System among fundable service categories, and schools may have separate per-school support. Funding eligibility is assessed against a specific proposal, not against a software product name, so the scope document matters more than the platform choice. We can prepare a scope that is structured to be assessed.

    What is the cheapest honest answer?

    For an organization that already has a working system, the cheapest decision is often to keep it for another renewal cycle and gather the data you need to compare properly. That is not a sales position; it is the correct answer for a substantial share of the organizations we speak to.
