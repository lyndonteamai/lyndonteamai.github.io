School LMS Funding in Hong Kong: Grant Scope and Implementation Planning

The Education Bureau has committed a one-off HK$500,000 per school under the Smart Education program, focused on AI in education. That is a meaningful sum, and it is also narrower than "money for an LMS". This page sets out the actual scope, the timings, and what a school should plan around.

School LMS Grant Eligibility and Funding Verification

We are an open-source LMS supplier, not a funding administrator, and we do not want to sell a school a project that depends on money it might not get. The Smart Education grant is real and it is significant, but it is directed at AI in education. If your project is "an LMS for teachers and students", the honest position is that the general LMS cost is not what this grant is designed to cover, and the plan needs to be built to stand without it. Where a school can genuinely use the grant is where AI has a real, evidenced role in learning — and that is a design conversation, not a procurement one.

School Moodle LMS Budget and Implementation Planning

01
Separate

School LMS Funding: Build the project so it works with no subsidy at all

This is the single most important discipline. Subsidy should accelerate a project you have already scoped and justified, not rescue one that does not make sense without it. If the plan only works at HK$500,000, it will not work at HK$300,000, and the school will be the one carrying the difference. Start from what the school needs, then apply the subsidy to whatever part of it genuinely qualifies.

02
Do not overreach

A conventional LMS is not an AI initiative

This sounds blunt, but it is better than a rejected application. Smart Education is directed at AI in education. Submitting a request for a standard learning management system under an AI grant is a poor fit, and we would rather say so than help a school build a proposal around a mismatch. Where AI genuinely belongs in the school’s plan — content assistance, adaptive practice, language support, analytics that inform teaching — the fit is real.

03
Understand the real cost

School LMS Maintenance and Unfunded Operating Costs

A one-off grant of HK$500,000 is a capital sum with a deadline, against a platform that has a running cost for as long as the school uses it. Hosting, configuration, content authoring, teacher training and the ongoing maintenance commitment all need to fit inside a multi-year budget. This is where the open-source model genuinely helps: the license is HK$0, so the grant is not competing with a per-seat renewal it can never win against.

School LMS Funding: Two things worth asking the Education Bureau

School LMS Funding Eligibility Review

  • What is the current submission window and deadline for your school year?
  • Does your intended project fall within the stated AI-in-education focus?
  • Is there a cap on the proportion of the grant that can go to third-party components?
  • What reporting and evidence is required at the end of the project period?

School LMS Budget and Grant Verification

  • Which costs are eligible, and which are the school’s own money?
  • What happens to what you bought if the project scales or stops?
  • What is the ongoing run cost after the grant period ends?
  • Does the school own the content, the code and the training records?

Hong Kong School LMS Funding: Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Smart Education grant per school?

HK$500,000, one-off. It was announced by the Education Bureau as part of a package directed at AI in education, usable across the 2025/26 to 2027/28 school years and up to 31 August 2028. We do not administer the scheme, so for the authoritative position including this year’s arrangements, please check the Education Bureau directly.

Can I use it to buy an LMS?

Not for a conventional learning management system, because that is not what the grant is focused on. We would rather be straight with you about that than help you build an application around a mismatch. If your project genuinely involves AI in a way that changes how teaching or learning works, that is a different conversation, and the right place to start is the Bureau’s current guidance on what qualifies.

What school funding is still open?

The Quality Education Fund continues to run e-Learning and eLAFP support schemes, with projects announced in batches, so a school learning project may qualify depending on the current call. The BUD Fund also remains open year-round with 1:3 matching and may support a technology deployment with a business rationale. The Technology Voucher Program stopped accepting new applications after 31 December 2024, and the Digital Transformation Support Pilot ended in May 2025, so any plan citing either is out of date.

Our school is small — is a full LMS realistic?

For a school, the maths is unusually favourable: student accounts are unlimited and carry no per-student charge, and the license is HK$0, so the platform cost does not scale with enrollment. What does scale is content, teacher time and the ongoing operations commitment. Most schools find it sensible to start with one workflow — student course access with a small number of staff using the administrative side — and expand once it is in use.

Does a school need technical staff to run this?

Not on day one, if a deployment option is chosen where someone else runs the infrastructure — the managed cloud or managed operations arrangements. What a school does need is an owner: a person accountable for the platform, the content and the annual review. Without that, the system degrades quietly, and the grant period is not long enough to absorb that.

Can the content and training records be ours?

Yes. The platform runs on an open-source stack under the GPL, so content, configuration and data are yours, and there is no per-student or per-teacher license to renegotiate when the school changes supplier or the grant ends. For a school that may need to hand the platform to a successor or a new operator, that matters more than it does in a single-company commercial arrangement.
