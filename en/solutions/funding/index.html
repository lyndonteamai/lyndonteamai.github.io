Funding

The platform is HK$0. The question is what the other lines will be funded.

Hong Kong funding changes constantly, and most of what circulates online about LMS funding is out of date. This page states which routes are actually open as of October 2026, with the source for each, and says plainly where we cannot help.

Ask about your scopeCost model first

Key facts about LMS funding in Hong Kong

The Technology Voucher closed to new applications
The Innovation and Technology Fund announced in December 2024 that the Technology Voucher Programme would stop accepting new applications after 31 December 2024. If a page tells you TVP is open, it is out of date.
The Digital Transformation Support Pilot has also closed
The pilot programme closed to new applications in May 2025. The government is redesigning it with AI and cybersecurity solutions added, with an additional HK$300 million, targeted for launch after Legislative Council consultation in the second half of 2026.
BUD remains open all year
The BUD Special Fund continues to accept applications year-round with 1:3 matching, general applications capped at HK$800,000 and Easy BUD at HK$150,000, and cumulative funding of HK$7 million.
Schools have a different route
The Education Bureau launched the "智啟學教" grant in late 2025 — a one-off HK$500,000 per school — but its stated focus is on school-based AI education, and the grant is for AI tools, resources and student activities rather than a general-purpose LMS.
The QEF e-Learning Ancillary Facilities Programme is real and open
QEF has funded 22 projects under the eLAFP, with project deliverables published in batches for schools to subscribe to with QEF sponsorship. These are curated education-specific platforms, not general LMS.
What we do not do
We are not a funding agent. We do not apply on your behalf, and we make no claim about what any application will be awarded.
Licence fee
HK$0. A funded project still has real costs — the scope document makes each one visible instead of hiding it inside a licence.

Why this page looks different from other funding pages

Most LMS funding pages online still describe the Technology Voucher as open, and treat the school grant as LMS funding. Both are out of date as of October 2026. We would rather tell you the route is closed than take an enquiry we cannot help with. The sources for every status above are cited in the FAQ below.

Where a zero-licence platform genuinely helps

01
Structure

Cost categories that are visibly separate

This is where an open-source platform helps concretely. Licence, hosting, configuration, migration, content and operations appear as distinct lines, each with its own justification. A single bundled price gives an assessor nothing to evaluate. Under a 1:3 matching scheme like BUD, the applicant also usually has to fund at least 25% of the project itself — and a visible cost breakdown is what makes that contribution defensible.

02
Structure

Outcomes, not features

Funded applications are assessed on what the project delivers and what changes as a result. "Deploy an LMS" is a purchase. "Move 800 shift-based staff onto a tracked certification cycle, with evidence of currency produced monthly" is a project with an outcome that can be assessed.

03
Structure

A maintenance plan written down

This is the specific question an assessor asks about an open-source project: who patches it, who upgrades it, who answers when it breaks. Stating the answer in the scope — with a named owner and a cost line — removes the concern that a zero-licence platform is really a deferred-maintenance problem.

04
Structure

Milestones with evidence attached

Each milestone should produce something an assessor can look at: a working environment, a reconciled data set, a pilot cohort, a published course library. We build deliverables that are demonstrable at each stage rather than at the end.

Two conversations, depending on who you are

For enterprises and SMEs

With TVP and the pilot closed, the realistic routes are BUD, and waiting for the redesigned pilot in the second half of 2026. The conversation is about your scope fitting a scheme that may or may not include what you need, and what evidence you will produce at each milestone.

  • BUD general versus Easy BUD, and your matching contribution
  • Whether the redesigned pilot will cover your category
  • Scope structure and milestone evidence
  • The split between funded and self-funded lines

For schools

School funding runs on different cycles and different priorities. The "智啟學教" grant is one-off and AI-focused; QEF remains the broader route for innovative learning projects, and eLAFP is a curated platform catalogue rather than an open procurement channel.

  • Whether your project genuinely delivers school-based AI education
  • QEF priority theme applications and deadlines
  • eLAFP subscription routes and deadlines
  • Long-term sustainability of the platform after the grant ends

Funding questions, with sources

Is the Technology Voucher still open?

No. The Innovation and Technology Fund announced in December 2024 that the Technology Voucher Programme would stop accepting new applications after 31 December 2024, having achieved its original purpose, with various policy bureaux launching more targeted schemes instead. Source: Innovation and Technology Fund news announcement, 13 December 2024 (news.gov.hk). The HKPC TVP page now carries a notice that new applications ceased after that date.

What about the Digital Transformation Support Pilot?

It closed to new applications in May 2025. In a Legislative Council reply dated 25 February 2026, the government said it is redesigning the programme — adding AI and cybersecurity solutions, with an additional HK$300 million — and targets launching the optimised version after consultation with LegCo in the second half of 2026. It also confirmed the pilot funded 8,799 SMEs at an average of about HK$49,000 each, concentrated in retail (55%) and food services (34%).

Which schemes are actually open right now?

BUD Special Fund accepts applications year-round with 1:3 matching. For schools, QEF remains open including its digital education priority theme, and the eLAFP publishes batches of funded deliverables for schools to subscribe to with QEF sponsorship. For AI compute, Cyberport’s Artificial Intelligence Supercomputing Centre scheme (AISS) subsidises up to 70% of AI compute pricing. The dates on this page are as of October 2026 and funding rules change — check the current scheme terms before you build a plan around them.

Does a HK$0 licence make a project easier to fund?

It does not automatically. Assessors look at the project and its outcomes. What a zero licence fee does is make the cost breakdown honest and legible — hosting, configuration, content and operations are each visible and each defensible, instead of one opaque licence number. Under a matching scheme, a clear breakdown is also what justifies your own contribution.

Can you guarantee we will get the funding?

No, and anyone who does is either mistaken or selling something. Approval is the assessing body's decision. We can make the scope strong and the write-up clear.

Will you sign an application as the vendor?

We can provide the technical and commercial information a vendor is expected to provide — architecture, deliverables, milestones, maintenance commitments and the cost breakdown. We do not write the applicant's business case for them, and we will not make representations outside what we can substantiate.

The "智啟學教" grant gives HK$500,000 per school. Does that cover your LMS?

Almost certainly not as a general LMS purchase. According to the Education Bureau, the programme's focus is supporting school-based AI education, and the grant is for procuring or subscribing to AI teaching tools and resources, and for funding student activities related to AI literacy. A general-purpose training management platform would only fit where the project genuinely delivers AI-enabled education. We will tell you honestly if that is not what you are building.

Do you work with funding consultants?

Yes. Many Hong Kong consultancies specialise in scheme applications, and we are comfortable working alongside one. If you already have an adviser, they can represent you; we supply the technical and commercial substance.

Start with the scope, not the application

Tell us what the project has to achieve and we will write the scope and the cost breakdown in the structure an assessor can read. If the numbers do not work, or if no open scheme fits, you will know before you spend time on an application.
