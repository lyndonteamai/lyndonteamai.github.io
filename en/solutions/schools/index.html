School Moodle LMS for Hong Kong: Courses, Assessments, and Free Licensing

OpenLMS is based on Moodle, a GPL-licensed learning management system. Moodle core provides courses, activities, quizzes, grades and learning records. Additional workflows require configuration, compatible plugins or scoped development.

Book a demoOpen-source base

Moodle Core vs. Configured Workflows and Extensions

This Astro website describes our Moodle-based service offering. The six solution areas are not six native Moodle modules. HRIS/SSO, sponsorship approvals, xAPI/LRS, certificates, and specialized reporting require configuration, compatible extensions, or scoped development. A working Moodle installation and acceptance evidence must be verified for your implementation. View the published Pricing page for subscription rates and separate at-cost infrastructure charges.

Hong Kong School LMS: Key Facts and Implementation Scope

Who it is for
Hong Kong schools, tutoring centers, academies and course providers, including students, parents, teachers and administrators.
What it covers
Structured course delivery, H5P interactive content, continuous assessment, certificates, transcripts and parent communication.
Student accounts
Unlimited. Accounts are not licensed per seat, so student numbers can grow without a license conversation.
Languages
Traditional Chinese (Hong Kong) is a first-class interface language, alongside Simplified Chinese and English.

HK$0 for the platform, with no per-student or per-school fee.

Hong Kong School LMS: Frequently Asked Questions

What does it cost per student?

There is no per-student license charge. Unlimited student accounts are supported on the same platform, and the platform license is HK$0. The costs that do exist are hosting or on-premises infrastructure, configuration, courseware and administration — and we quote them as separate lines, because a per-student number would misrepresent where the money goes.

Can teachers author content themselves?

Yes. H5P authoring runs in the browser, so interactive content — video with interaction points, quizzes, presentations, branching scenarios — can be built by school staff without a development toolchain. That is usually the difference between a content library that grows and one that stalls after the initial set.

Can examinations run on the school network?

Yes. Examination computers can operate over a private local network with no public internet during the session, with isolated workstations, controlled exam loading and result collection. This is often a requirement for internal school assessments rather than an optional extra.

What about parent and teacher communication?

Teacher-facing views cover class enrollment, assignment, progress and marking, and certificates and transcripts are produced as PDF. Parent communication is a workflow rather than a built-in messaging product, and we scope it as configuration against whatever channel you already use.

Is the QEF Smart Education School Grant relevant to us?

Potentially, and it needs to be checked against your own situation. The grant provides a one-off HK$500,000 per school and is focused on AI in education, so it is aimed at AI-enablement projects rather than at general LMS purchase. It has to be applied for against a specific proposal, not against a software product, and eligibility is assessed by the funding body. We can structure a scope that is ready to be assessed, but we cannot tell you that you will be awarded it.

Will this work for a small school with limited technical support?

It can, but it depends on who runs the platform. Managed hosting is usually the sensible answer for a small school, and our scoping includes being honest about whether your internal capacity is enough to run it yourselves. We will not recommend self-hosting to an organization that nobody can maintain.
